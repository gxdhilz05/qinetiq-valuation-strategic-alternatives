# Comparable-company selection

The peer set should resemble QinetiQ's government-contracting, defence-services and technology economics. No listed company is a perfect match.

## Core peers

| Company | Why it is useful | Main limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Babcock International | UK defence engineering, support, training and major government exposure | More asset-intensive and exposed to naval/nuclear support |
| Serco | Government-services contract economics and UK public-sector exposure | Much broader than defence and less technology-focused |
| Leidos | Large US defence, intelligence and government-technology services provider | Much larger and almost entirely US-focused |
| CACI International | US defence and intelligence technology/services exposure | Higher US growth profile and different contract mix |
| Booz Allen Hamilton | US government technology, cyber and consulting model | Often receives a premium multiple and has more consulting exposure |

## Secondary reference peers

- Parsons: US defence and infrastructure technology.
- Chemring: UK defence technology exposure, but more product/manufacturing-heavy.
- Cohort: smaller UK defence electronics and technology company.
- BAE Systems, RTX and Lockheed Martin: useful sector references, but not core peers because they manufacture major platforms and products.

## How the set will be used

- Use the five core peers for the main trading-comparables range.
- Show secondary peers separately as a reasonableness check.
- Compare growth, margins, geography, contract mix and scale before applying a multiple.
- Do not mechanically apply the median if QinetiQ's business quality or US risk differs materially.

We will collect current market values and forecast multiples only when building the comparable-company model, because those figures change with share prices and analyst estimates.
