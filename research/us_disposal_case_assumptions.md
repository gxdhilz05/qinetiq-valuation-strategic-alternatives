# US disposal case

## Purpose

This case estimates the value to QinetiQ shareholders if the US operation is sold and QinetiQ retains EMEA Services and the Other Products businesses. It is an illustrative strategic case, not a prediction that a transaction will occur or a statement of management guidance.

## Scope of the sale

QinetiQ reported £393.4m of FY26 Global Solutions revenue, comprising approximately £290.7m from the United States and £102.7m from Other Products. Other Products includes QinetiQ Target Systems and is retained in this case. Therefore, the model does not remove the whole Global Solutions segment.

The FY26 Global Solutions operating profit of £35.6m is not disclosed separately between the US and Other Products. The case assumes a 7.5% FY26 US operating margin, equivalent to £21.8m of US operating profit. The residual £13.8m is allocated to Other Products, implying a 13.4% margin. This is a modelling allocation that reconciles exactly to the reported segment total.

## Transaction assumptions

| Assumption | Base case | Rationale |
|---|---:|---|
| US revenue sold | £290.7m | FY26 company disclosure. |
| Sale EV / revenue | 1.00x | Illustrative value for a stabilised but lower-growth, lower-margin defence-services portfolio. |
| Gross sale proceeds | £290.7m | US revenue multiplied by the sale multiple. |
| Proceeds leakage | 10.0% | Combined allowance for transaction fees, separation costs, tax and working-capital adjustments. |
| Net sale proceeds | £261.6m | Gross proceeds after the 10% allowance. |
| Transaction timing | Immediate | The sale is treated as effective at the valuation date to compare strategic alternatives on a clean basis. |

The 1.00x revenue multiple is materially below the approximately 1.98x revenue multiple that QinetiQ paid for Avantus in 2022. Avantus was then growing at double-digit rates with double-digit margins; QinetiQ's current US portfolio has weaker growth, lower visibility and lower estimated profitability. The acquisition multiple is therefore a reference point, not a price target.

## Retained-business assumptions

EMEA Services uses the same revenue and margin assumptions as the base case. Other Products starts from £102.7m of FY26 revenue and the implied 13.4% FY26 margin.

| Assumption | FY27E | FY28E | FY29E | FY30E | FY31E |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Other Products revenue growth | 5.0% | 5.0% | 4.0% | 4.0% | 3.0% |
| Other Products operating margin | 13.5% | 14.0% | 14.5% | 14.5% | 14.5% |
| Stranded overhead cost (£m) | 6.0 | 4.0 | 2.0 | - | - |

Other Products grew 6% on a constant-currency basis in FY26. The forecast begins at 5% and tapers to 3%, avoiding an assumption that the latest growth rate continues indefinitely. The margin rises gradually from the inferred FY26 level as product revenue grows.

The stranded-overhead line represents group and shared costs that cannot be removed immediately after a sale. It falls from £6m in FY27 to zero by FY30. This prevents the model from assuming that every cost allocated to the US business disappears on day one.

The retained business uses the same 26.5% cash tax rate, 5.0% D&A/revenue, 4.5% CapEx/revenue, 1.0% working-capital investment rate, 8.6% WACC and 2.0% terminal-growth rate as the base case. Keeping these inputs unchanged isolates the strategic effect of removing the US operation and receiving sale proceeds.

## Valuation result

| £m except per-share data | Disposal case |
|---|---:|
| PV of explicit-period UFCF | 708.0 |
| PV of terminal value | 2,031.1 |
| Retained-business enterprise value | 2,739.1 |
| Add: net sale proceeds | 261.6 |
| Less: group net debt | (159.1) |
| Equity value | 2,841.6 |
| Implied value per share | £5.44 |
| Difference from base case | (£0.07) |

At a 1.00x revenue sale multiple, the disposal case is worth approximately £5.44 per share, compared with £5.51 in the base case. The result is about £0.07 per share lower because the £261.6m net proceeds do not fully replace the present value of the US cash flows that are sold.

## Sale-multiple sensitivity

| Sale EV / revenue | Net proceeds (£m) | Implied value per share | Difference from base |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.75x | 196.2 | £5.32 | (£0.19) |
| 0.875x | 228.9 | £5.38 | (£0.13) |
| 1.00x | 261.6 | £5.44 | (£0.07) |
| 1.125x | 294.3 | £5.51 | (£0.00) |
| 1.25x | 327.0 | £5.57 | £0.06 |

The break-even sale multiple is approximately 1.13x FY26 US revenue after the 10% proceeds allowance. A price above that level creates more value than the retain-with-limited-improvement base case; a lower price destroys value relative to that case.

## Interview explanation

“I treated the divestment as a sum-of-parts valuation. I removed only the £291m US operation, retained the £103m Other Products portfolio, and valued the remaining EMEA plus products cash flows using the same WACC and terminal-growth assumptions as the base case. I added net sale proceeds after a 10% allowance for fees, separation, tax and working-capital adjustments, then deducted group net debt. At a 1.0x revenue sale multiple the result was £5.44 per share, slightly below the £5.51 base case. The break-even price was about 1.13x revenue, so the recommendation depends mainly on achievable sale proceeds and execution risk rather than disposal automatically creating value.”
