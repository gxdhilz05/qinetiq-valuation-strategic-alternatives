# Trading comparable companies

## Purpose

The trading-comparables analysis values QinetiQ using the current enterprise-value multiples of listed defence, government-services and mission-technology companies. It is an independent market cross-check on the DCF rather than a replacement for the cash-flow valuation.

Market capitalisations are measured on 16 September 2026. Financial data use each company's latest completed annual reporting period available on that date. Enterprise value is calculated as market capitalisation plus debt less cash. Currency conversion is unnecessary because each multiple divides amounts reported in the same currency.

## Peer selection

| Group | Companies | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| UK defence and engineering | Babcock, Chemring, Cohort | UK-listed exposure to defence budgets, engineering, products and mission-critical services. |
| Government services | Serco | Comparable long-term government contracting and service delivery, but less defence-technology intensity. |
| US defence and intelligence services | Leidos, CACI, Booz Allen Hamilton, SAIC | Comparable customers, cleared workforces, cyber/intelligence capabilities and US federal contracting exposure. |

Very large prime contractors such as BAE Systems, Lockheed Martin and RTX were excluded because their scale, platform manufacturing, programme risk and capital intensity differ materially from QinetiQ. Palantir and other high-growth software companies were excluded because their growth and margin profiles would distort the peer range.

Babcock operating profit is the FY26 normalised headline result before the one-off £140m Type 31 charge. Chemring, Cohort and Serco use their reported underlying or adjusted operating-profit measures. The US companies use GAAP operating income from their latest Form 10-K. Because definitions are not perfectly uniform, EV/revenue is the cleanest top-line comparison and EV/operating profit is the profitability cross-check.

## Implied QinetiQ valuation

| Method | Peer median | QinetiQ metric | Implied enterprise value | Less net debt | Implied equity value | Implied value / share |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| EV / revenue | 1.14x | £1,922.6m | £2,200.1m | £159.1m | £2,041.0m | £3.91 |
| EV / operating profit | 13.41x | £217.9m | £2,922.7m | £159.1m | £2,763.6m | £5.29 |

The revenue method produces a lower value because several service-heavy peers operate at lower margins than QinetiQ. The operating-profit method better recognises QinetiQ's 11.3% FY26 underlying margin and therefore provides the more meaningful trading cross-check. Its £5.29 per-share result is below, but reasonably close to, the £5.51 base-case DCF.

## Interview explanation

“I chose peers that overlap with QinetiQ's defence customers, government-contracting model and mission-technology capabilities, then used current market capitalisations and latest completed annual financials. I calculated enterprise value as equity value plus net debt and compared EV to revenue and operating profit. The median revenue multiple implied £3.91 per share, while the median operating-profit multiple implied £5.29. The difference is driven by margins: QinetiQ earns a higher margin than several service-heavy peers, so I treat the EBIT-based result as the better trading cross-check rather than averaging the two mechanically.”
