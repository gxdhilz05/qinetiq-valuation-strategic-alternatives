# Global Solutions restructuring case

## Purpose of the case

This case asks whether QinetiQ should retain Global Solutions and carry out a further targeted restructuring instead of accepting limited improvement or selling the US business.

It is an incremental case. QinetiQ has already completed a substantial US restructuring, so the model must not count the benefits of the completed programme for a second time. The proposed case assumes a smaller second phase focused on contract selection, overhead reduction and better execution.

## What has already happened

- Global Solutions revenue fell from £453.9m in FY25 to £393.4m in FY26 as QinetiQ exited non-core and low-margin work.
- The Global Solutions margin recovered from 3.6% to 9.0% as the US business stabilised and earlier one-off charges reduced.
- The US Federal IT portfolio was sold for approximately $31m in September 2025.
- QinetiQ reduced its cost base and Global Solutions year-end employees fell from 1,500 to 1,165, a reduction of approximately 22%.
- Group restructuring costs and associated impacts were £64.5m in FY25 and £30.2m in FY26. QinetiQ stated that the FY26 US element included further non-cash charges and that no further material US charges arose in the second half.
- The FY26 Global Solutions segment consisted of approximately £291m of US revenue and £103m of other products, so the assumptions below apply to the whole segment rather than treating every pound of Global Solutions revenue as US revenue.
- Global Solutions had only 59% of FY27 revenue under contract at 31 March 2026, compared with 67% one year earlier. Funded backlog fell to £279.1m and US unfunded backlog fell to £342.1m.
- As of the FY27 first-quarter update, management was still reviewing all strategic options for the US business and expected to provide another update with the interim results on 12 November 2026.

The segment has previously achieved margins of approximately 10.5%, but performance has been volatile. This supports modelling a recovery towards 10-11%, not an immediate move to EMEA-level profitability.

## Proposed restructuring assumptions

| Assumption | FY27E | FY28E | FY29E | FY30E | FY31E |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Global Solutions revenue growth | (4.0%) | 0.0% | 4.0% | 5.0% | 4.0% |
| Global Solutions operating margin | 7.5% | 9.0% | 10.0% | 10.5% | 11.0% |
| Gross restructuring cash costs (£m) | 15.0 | 10.0 | - | - | - |
| After-tax restructuring cash costs (£m) | 11.0 | 7.4 | - | - | - |
| Implementation status | Main actions | Completion | Benefits building | Fully implemented | Steady state |

The after-tax cost uses the model's 26.5% cash tax rate. The gross costs are estimates for this hypothetical strategy, not management guidance.

### Revenue growth

FY27 revenue falls 4% because a further restructuring would require QinetiQ to exit more unattractive contracts and would distract the organisation during implementation. FY28 is flat as the portfolio stabilises. Growth returns from FY29 as the business focuses on space and missile defence, maritime systems, persistent surveillance, sensing and autonomy. The forecast does not assume that a stronger defence market automatically produces immediate contract wins.

### Operating margin

The margin falls to 7.5% in FY27 because of disruption and lower utilisation, before recovering as recurring overhead savings and better contract selection take effect. The FY29-FY30 assumptions move back towards the 10.4-10.5% margins achieved in FY23-FY24. The 11.0% FY31 margin is slightly above the historical peak but remains below the EMEA margin and assumes a smaller, better-quality portfolio.

### Restructuring cash costs

The case assumes £25m of incremental gross cash costs: £15m in FY27 and £10m in FY28. This is smaller than the recent reported restructuring charges because the major rightsizing programme has already been completed. No additional large headcount reduction is assumed because QinetiQ has not provided evidence supporting one.

## How the case will enter UFCF

```text
Restructuring-case NOPAT = EBIT x (1 - cash tax rate)
Restructuring-case UFCF before one-off costs = NOPAT + D&A - CapEx - change in NWC
Restructuring-case UFCF = UFCF before one-off costs - after-tax restructuring cash costs
```

The one-off costs must be shown separately rather than hidden inside the operating margin. This makes the initial cash sacrifice and the later recurring benefit visible and prevents double counting.

## Decision test

The restructuring case creates value only if the present value of the later improvement in profit and UFCF exceeds the present value of the £25m gross implementation cost and the cash lost through short-term revenue disruption. The DCF calculation should determine the answer; the assumptions should not be chosen to force restructuring to win.

## DCF result

The formula-driven model applies the same 8.6% WACC, 2.0% terminal-growth rate, £159.1m net debt and 522.1m diluted shares as the base case. This isolates the value effect of the operating assumptions rather than mixing the strategic comparison with different valuation parameters.

| £m except per-share data | Base case | Restructuring case | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| FY27 UFCF | 174.2 | 159.5 | (14.7) |
| FY31 UFCF | 210.4 | 212.8 | 2.4 |
| Present value of explicit-period UFCF | 787.8 | 768.1 | (19.7) |
| Present value of terminal value | 2,247.5 | 2,272.8 | 25.3 |
| Enterprise value | 3,035.3 | 3,040.9 | 5.5 |
| Equity value | 2,876.2 | 2,881.8 | 5.5 |
| Implied value per share | £5.51 | £5.52 | £0.01 |

On these assumptions, restructuring creates only approximately £5.5m of enterprise value, or £0.01 per share. The result is effectively neutral: the higher long-run margin and FY31 cash flow are largely offset by the £25m gross cash cost and weaker near-term revenue. This is strategically useful because it shows that a second restructuring phase is not an obvious value-creation answer and supports retaining a sale or other strategic alternative as a live option.

## Interview explanation

“I treated restructuring as an incremental second phase because QinetiQ had already reduced headcount, disposed of Federal IT and exited low-return work. I therefore assumed temporary revenue disruption and £25m of further gross cash costs, followed by a gradual margin recovery towards the segment's historical 10-11% range. I kept the one-off costs separate from the recurring margin so I could compare the present value of the turnaround benefits with the upfront cash investment without double counting.”
