Qinetiq:

It is a defence and security technology company, earning money by providing:
-engineering services
-testing and evaluation of missiles, aircraft
-military training
-mission support
-intelligence and cyber operations
-research and development
According to FY26 revenue came mostly from service contracts(96%).

History:
Originated from DERA(Defence Evaluation and Research Agency) in 2001. When DERA was divided into Qinetiq and Dstl, highly sensitive activites remained inside government and became Dtsl, most of the remaining activities became Qinetiq, which was subsequently privatised. In 2006 it was listed on the London Stock Exchange. 

Due to origination from DERA, Qinetiq inherited specialist technical employees like researchers and scientists; had prior security knowledge and clearances; maintained experience working with MOD; inherited well established customer relationships; responsibilities connected with important test facilities.

Customers' use of Qinetiq:
Military systems need to be tested safely and the government requires trusted, security-cleared suppliers. Qinetiq has these specialist scientists and engineers, it also has access to infrastructure that would be expensive to reproduce so replacing with another provider can create technical and security risks.

Advantage of these customers: governments can offer large, long-term contracts and have reliable access to funding. 
Risk : Qinetiq is highly dependent on government properties, procurement decisions, contrat timing. 

Combination of Advantage and risk: if government changes its spending plans then QInetiq would not immediately lose all its revenue due to future signed contracts and funded backlog but longer-term growth could weaken.

Long Term Partnering Agreement (LTPA) : 
An important agreement between Qinetiq and MOD under which:
-Q delivers test,trials,training and evaluation services
-work is conducted across 16 core MOD-owned sites
-original framework begain in 2003 and in May 2025 it was extended for 5 years, worth about £1.54bn plus Q's reported order intake of an additional £166m of associated investment so total of £1.7bn.

The LTPA is important because it provides:
-Revenue visibility.
-A strong MOD relationship.
-Access to specialist facilities.
-A significant barrier to competitors.
Facilities are generally MOD owned.

How Q's contracts become revenue:
Opportunity : contract award : order intake : funded backlog : work performed : revenue : cash

Order intake
New contracted work booked during the year.

Funded backlog
Contracted work that has customer funding but has not yet been recognised as revenue. This is not automatically revenue because Q must still perform the work, employees and facilities have costs, different contracts have different margins, delivery can be delayed, contracts can be changed or cancelled. 

Revenue
Recorded as QinetiQ performs its contractual obligations.

Cash
May be collected before or after revenue is recognised.

Underlying versus statutory performance:
QinetiQ presents both statutory (official, legally mandated financial results) and underlying performance (management's view of core, ongoing business performance).

In FY25 underlying segment operating profit was £185.4m and Statutory operating loss was £90.5m, major reason of difference was an impairment associated with US business meaning accoutning value of US business reduced because management no longer expected it to generate as much value as originally forecast.

10. US underperformance
The US operation has experienced:
-Slower contract awards.
-Budget and procurement uncertainty.
-Changes in government spending priorities.
-Weak contract execution.
-Lower-return work.
-Previous acquisition impairments.

Management has responded by:
-Selling Federal IT.
-Cutting costs.
-Exiting lower-return contracts.
-Restructuring the operation.
-Beginning a strategic review.

US profitability improved in FY26, but revenue fell. Therefore, we cannot yet conclude that the business has achieved a genuine operational recovery.

Strategic alternatives:
1. Retain US business: will keep access to US defence market, preserve potential future recovery, avoid selling at a depressed valuation, maintain geographical diversification. However, it will continue execution risk, there is possibility of further impairments, management time and capital will remain tied up and and further US weakness may continue depressing Q's overall valuation.
2. Retain and restructure further: could improve margins before any future sale, allow Q to exit poor contracts, could create greater value if the turnaround succeeds. However, there will be additional restructuring costs, recovery can take several years. there is no guarantee that revenue or orders will recover and investors may remain sceptical.
3. Sell US business: removing the weaker operation will release cash and simplify the group allow management to focus on EMEA and may reveal the value of higher quality core business. However, selling during weakness might produce a lower price, Q could lose future US growth, tax and transaction costs reduce proceeds, a low sale could confirm that previous acquisitions destroyed value.

Best assessment would be to recommend the option that produces best combination of shareholder value, strategic logic, risk, cash generation and execution feasibility.

Commercial Awareness:
Higher UK and allied defence budgets increase the potential amount of work done but this increase in defence budget doesn't necessarily mean Q's revenue will increase because money must be allocated to relevant programmes and Q must win the contract and then perform the work.
Advancements in tech complexity of autonomous systems, drones, cyber and electronic warfare should increase demand for testing, assurance, training and systems integration. However, government contracting is slow, delayed funding or approvals can delay orders, revenue, cash collection. Due to inflation, wage costs for labour and even labour shortages could delay delivery, increase costs and reduce margins. Recently interest costs have increased too which will increase interest expense on floating rate debt, increase WACC, reduce DCF valuation. Also due to forex, Q reports in pounds but earn in US and Australian dollars, currency movements can change reported financial results even when udnerlying local operations have not changed. So we must distinguish between reported growth, organic growth and constant currency growth.
