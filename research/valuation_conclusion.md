# Valuation conclusion and strategic recommendation

## Valuation conclusion

The most relevant valuation outputs are the operating-profit trading comparable and the two retain-case DCFs.

| Method | Implied value per share | Use in conclusion |
|---|---:|---|
| Trading comparables: revenue | £3.91 | Downside cross-check. It gives insufficient credit for QinetiQ's higher operating margin. |
| Trading comparables: operating profit | £5.29 | Core public-market cross-check. |
| Base-case DCF | £5.51 | Core intrinsic valuation. |
| Restructuring-case DCF | £5.52 | Core strategic alternative. |
| US disposal at 1.00x revenue | £5.44 | Conservative transaction case. |
| US disposal at 1.81x precedent median | £5.85 | Conditional transaction upside, not a base case. |

The core valuation range is £5.29-£5.52 per share. An illustrative central value of approximately £5.40 gives 50% weight to the operating-profit trading result, 25% to the base DCF and 25% to the restructuring DCF. This gives equal total weight to public-market evidence and intrinsic valuation without including a transaction that may not occur.

The £5.40 central value represents approximately 16% upside to the research-date share price of £4.66. The conclusion should still be presented as a range because approximately 74% of base-case enterprise value comes from terminal value.

## Strategic recommendation

QinetiQ should continue improving the US operation while maintaining a competitive sale process. This preserves both alternatives and may improve buyer confidence and sale proceeds.

The model does not support a forced sale near 1.00x revenue:

| Sale EV / revenue | Implied value per share | Difference from base DCF | Interpretation |
|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 1.00x | £5.44 | (£0.07) | Destroys value versus retaining the business. |
| 1.13x | £5.51 | £0.00 | Mathematical break-even after the 10% proceeds allowance. |
| 1.25x | £5.57 | £0.06 | Creates limited value; reasonable minimum discussion point. |
| 1.50x | £5.69 | £0.18 | More meaningful buffer for transaction and execution risk. |
| 1.81x | £5.85 | £0.34 | Precedent-median upside reference. |

The recommended decision rule is:

- do not sell near 1.00x revenue;
- treat 1.13x as economic break-even, not an acceptable target;
- require at least approximately 1.25x, with a price closer to 1.50x preferable after considering tax, separation costs, stranded overhead and transaction certainty;
- retain and continue improving the business if bids remain below that range.

The restructuring DCF is only £0.01 per share above the base DCF. Therefore, a second restructuring programme is not valuable purely because of the calculated uplift. Its main benefit is preserving strategic flexibility, improving contract quality and potentially strengthening QinetiQ's negotiating position with buyers.

## Main risks

1. **Terminal-value dependence.** Approximately 74% of base-case enterprise value comes from cash flows beyond FY31, making the DCF sensitive to WACC and perpetual growth.
2. **US execution.** Global Solutions has volatile margins, weaker contracted revenue visibility and lower backlog than EMEA Services.
3. **Transaction deductions.** Tax, fees, separation costs, working-capital adjustments and stranded overhead could exceed the assumed 10% allowance.
4. **Comparable-company differences.** The peer group includes companies with different margins, business mixes and accounting definitions.
5. **Precedent premiums.** Historical transactions may include control premiums and expected synergies and several targets had stronger growth or more differentiated technology.
6. **Market-date sensitivity.** Share prices, market capitalisations and WACC inputs should be refreshed before an interview, presentation or investment decision.

## Interview explanation

“I triangulated the valuation rather than relying on one method. The EBIT trading comparable implied £5.29 per share, while the base and restructuring DCFs implied £5.51 and £5.52. That gave me a core range of £5.29 to £5.52 and an illustrative central value of about £5.40, roughly 16% above the research-date share price. The revenue comparable was lower at £3.91 because several peers have weaker margins, so I treated it as a downside check rather than the main answer.

For the US strategy, selling at 1.0x revenue produced £5.44 per share, below the base DCF. The mathematical break-even multiple was about 1.13x, but that offers no buffer for execution risk. I would continue improving the US business while running a competitive sale process, and only recommend a sale at least around 1.25x revenue, preferably closer to 1.5x. If buyers will not pay that range, retaining the business is more defensible.”
