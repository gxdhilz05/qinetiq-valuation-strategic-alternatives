# QinetiQ company background

## 1. What the company is

QinetiQ is a defence and security technology company. It mainly earns revenue by supplying specialist engineering, test and evaluation, training, mission support, intelligence and research services to governments. It does sell some products, but approximately 96% of FY26 revenue came from service contracts. [S001, S004, S006]

It is therefore not best understood as a manufacturer of complete platforms such as fighter aircraft or warships. It helps customers develop, test, assure, train with and operate complex technology.

## 2. Why it exists

QinetiQ was created in 2001 when the UK Government split the Defence Evaluation and Research Agency (DERA). The most sensitive work remained in government as Dstl; most of the remaining activities, including major test and evaluation capabilities, became QinetiQ. QinetiQ was later privatised and listed in 2006. [S005]

This history matters because it helps explain QinetiQ's technical expertise, long-standing Ministry of Defence relationship and access to specialist facilities.

## 3. What it sells

Its capabilities can be grouped into:

- Engineering services: technical advice, systems engineering and assurance.
- Mission support and operations: training, readiness, intelligence and operational support.
- Test and training: facilities and expertise used to test and evaluate equipment safely.
- Research and development: work in areas such as autonomy, cyber, sensing and directed energy.

FY26 revenue by capability was approximately 34% engineering services, 27% mission support and operations, 26% test and training and 13% research and development. [S006]

## 4. Customers and concentration

FY26 revenue was concentrated:

- UK government: £1,290.4m, or 67%.
- US government: £248.8m, or 13%.
- Other customers: £383.4m, or 20%.
- By location, 74% of revenue came from the UK and 15% from the US. [S006]

Government customers provide long contracts, high barriers to entry and relatively visible demand. The downside is dependence on budgets, procurement decisions and contract timing. Higher defence budgets help only if QinetiQ wins relevant work and then performs it.

EMEA Services is the stronger core business. It includes UK defence and intelligence work plus Australia and parts of Europe. Revenue and margin grew in FY26.

Global Solutions principally contains the US operation and Target Systems. Its FY26 revenue fell, but profit improved after cost reductions, disposal of the Federal IT business and withdrawal from lower-return work. Lower revenue alongside a higher margin can therefore reflect deliberate pruning and restructuring rather than improved demand. [S001, S004]

## 6. Contract economics

The commercial chain is:

**Pipeline → contract award/order intake → funded backlog → work performed → revenue recognised → cash collected**

- Order intake is new contracted work booked during the period.
- Funded backlog is contracted work with customer funding that has not yet been recognised as revenue.
- Revenue is recognised as QinetiQ satisfies the contract's performance obligations.
- Cash may arrive before or after revenue, creating contract liabilities or contract assets/receivables.

Backlog is not profit or cash. It is delivered over time, carries costs and may have different margins. Of the £4,421.4m FY26 funded backlog, £1,347.2m was expected to become FY27 revenue; the remainder was scheduled for later years. [S004, S006]

The Long Term Partnering Agreement (LTPA) is a strategic framework under which QinetiQ delivers test, trials, training and evaluation at 16 core MOD-owned sites. It began in 2003 and was extended in May 2025 for five years. The contract extension was £1.54bn; FY26 order reporting also included £166m of planned investment, producing the larger £1.7bn figure. [S003, S004]

## 7. Competitive advantages

- Specialist facilities and infrastructure that are expensive and difficult to replicate.
- Security clearances, technical expertise and trusted customer relationships.
- Long-term contracts and deep integration into critical programmes.
- High switching risk for customers when safety, national security and certification matter.

These are barriers to entry, not a guarantee of growth. QinetiQ must still win work, recruit skilled staff and deliver contracts profitably.

Revenue grew strongly from FY22 to FY24, helped by acquisitions as well as organic growth, then flattened. FY25 profit was damaged by the US operation; FY26 margins recovered. Net debt remained modest at 0.5x EBITDA in FY26. [S001, S006]

FY25 statutory operating loss was £90.5m despite £185.4m of underlying segment operating profit, principally because the statutory result included a large US goodwill impairment and restructuring-related adjustments. A non-cash impairment does not use current-year cash, but it signals that earlier acquisition expectations were too optimistic. [S004, S006]

## 9. The US strategic question

The US business faced slower awards, budget pressure, changing customer priorities and weak execution. Management sold Federal IT, reduced costs and exited lower-return contracts. Its margin recovered, but revenue declined and the business remains under strategic review. [S001, S002, S004]

The choices we will assess are:

1. Retain and restructure it.
2. Sell it.
3. Partially dispose of or separate it.

The decision matters because the US operation can depress the valuation placed on the stronger EMEA business. A sale could simplify the group and release cash, but a low sale price could crystallise the loss from past acquisitions. Retaining it preserves US growth exposure but leaves execution risk.

## 10. Why QinetiQ is the right project company

- It connects directly to aeronautical engineering through testing, systems engineering, mission systems and defence technology.
- It has enough public data for a proper model.
- Its two segments have contrasting performance, so forecasting requires judgement.
- The live US review creates an authentic advisory question.
- It supports DCF, trading comparables, sum-of-the-parts valuation and strategic alternatives analysis.
- It is less generic than analysing a large platform manufacturer, while remaining understandable.
