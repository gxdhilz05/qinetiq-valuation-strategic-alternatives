# Precedent transactions

## Purpose

Precedent transactions show what buyers have paid for control of comparable defence, intelligence and government-technology assets. They include control premiums and expected synergies, so they normally sit above unaffected public-market multiples. In this project their main use is to test the 1.00x revenue sale assumption in the US-disposal case.

The median is based only on the four transactions with disclosed or independently verifiable revenue. It is not applied as the base assumption without adjustment: Azure Summit and Avantus had stronger growth, differentiated technology and strategic scarcity, while PAE was a lower-margin services asset. LinQuest's reported multiple is included as an EBITDA cross-check but excluded from the revenue median because revenue was not disclosed in the announcement.

## Implication for the QinetiQ US disposal case

| Metric | Existing disposal case | Precedent-median reference |
|---|---:|---:|
| Sale EV / revenue | 1.00x | 1.81x |
| FY26 US revenue | £290.7m | £290.7m |
| Gross proceeds | £290.7m | £527.6m |
| Net proceeds after 10% leakage | £261.6m | £474.8m |
| Implied value per share | £5.44 | £5.85 |

The existing 1.00x assumption is conservative relative to the 1.81x disclosed-transaction median and below the model's approximately 1.13x break-even multiple. However, precedent prices include control and synergy value and several targets were faster-growing or more technology-rich than QinetiQ's currently challenged US portfolio. The evidence supports a wider negotiation range, not simply replacing the base case with 1.81x.

A sensible interpretation is:

- below 1.13x: disposal destroys value versus the base DCF after the 10% proceeds allowance;
- around 1.13x–1.50x: plausible value-creating range if the portfolio is stabilised and separation risk is manageable;
- around the 1.81x median: upside reference requiring strong buyer competition, credible growth and limited diligence discounts.

## Interview explanation

“I selected transactions involving US defence, intelligence, cyber, space and government-services assets. I calculated EV-to-revenue where revenue was disclosed and kept reported EBITDA multiples as a separate cross-check. The disclosed revenue-multiple median was about 1.81x, versus the model's 1.00x sale assumption and a 1.13x break-even price. I did not automatically use 1.81x because precedent deals include control premiums and synergies and some targets had superior growth or technology. Instead, I used the evidence to show that 1.00x is conservative and that the disposal could create value if QinetiQ attracts a price materially above 1.13x.”
